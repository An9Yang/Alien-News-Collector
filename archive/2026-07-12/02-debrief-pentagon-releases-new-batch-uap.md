## Original (English)

**By Micah Hanks — The Debrief · Published July 10, 2026**

The United States Department of War (DOW) released its latest collection of imagery and other materials related to unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) on Friday, July 10, 2026. It is the fourth official release under what the DOW has dubbed the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE), and the latest tranche of files includes several new, previously unseen U.S. military videos depicting unidentified aerial objects acquired by the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO).

"Today, the Department of War is publishing the fourth release of declassified and historical Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) files as part of the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE)," said Assistant to the Secretary of War for Public Affairs and Chief Pentagon Spokesman Sean Parnell.

"The collection continues to be housed on WAR.GOV/UFO, and the Department will release additional files on a rolling basis," Parnell added.

### What is in the release

The new videos show small, blurry objects captured with multi-sensor electro-optical targeting capabilities from fighter aircraft or unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and in some cases, imaging systems onboard civilian aircraft. To date, none of the videos or other documentation made available under PURSUE has provided clear examples of any objects attributable to unknown aircraft, or objects exhibiting unusual or advanced technologies.

Much of the legitimately new material focuses on unresolved UAP cases — incidents in which the government has been unable to determine the nature of the observed phenomena due to insufficient data or other limitations.

In addition to the video footage, several historical documents from as early as the 1940s were included with the release, the majority of which were previously made available in past releases by various U.S. government agencies.

### Notable cases

Among the unresolved reports included with the fourth batch:

- **"DOW-UAP-PR030, Unresolved UAP Report, Middle East, 2023"** shows two areas of contrast transiting the sensor field-of-view.
- **"DOW-UAP-PR112, Unresolved UAP Report, Eastern United States, 2019"** — the observer reportedly described the object as exhibiting "flight characteristics unlike anything [the observer] had seen in 28 years for the U.S. Air Force."
- **"DOW-UAP-PR116, Unresolved UAP Report, Atlantic Ocean, 2020"** includes descriptions of an object with a "darker, maroonish color, approximately 12-15 feet in height" that appeared similar to a "large, somewhat deformed balloon."

Footage reportedly collected over the western United States in 2020 is notable due to its similarity to the famous "Tic Tac" imagery from 2004, captured by F/A-18 Super Hornet pilot Chad Underwood off the Baja California coast. However, like others in recent PURSUE releases, the footage provides little additional context, and it is unclear what the object may be.

One series of historical NASA images included with the new PURSUE release originates from the 1996 STS-80 mission, featuring photographs obtained by astronauts aboard the Space Shuttle Columbia that convey a small triangular or cone-shaped object. During STS-80, between November 19 and December 7, 1996, astronauts captured a series of three images of an unidentified object in low-Earth orbit, with the object visible near the center of the frame in the first photograph, to the right of the limb of the Earth. It is unclear what the object in the photograph may be, although several observations of allegedly unusual phenomena occurred during STS-80. The most common interpretation of these objects is that they were ice particles outgassed from the spacecraft, whose highly reflective surfaces could produce the optical effects captured by the shuttle's cameras.

### What comes next

The collection continues to be housed on WAR.GOV/UFO, and the Department of War will release additional files on a rolling basis. The DOW and its agency partners "are actively working on the next release of UAP files," which can be expected to appear on the DOW website in the coming weeks.

Since the site's launch on May 8, 2026, WAR.GOV/UFO has received over 1.7 billion hits worldwide.

## 简体中文译稿

**作者：麦卡·汉克斯（Micah Hanks）— The Debrief · 2026 年 7 月 10 日发布**

美国战争部（U.S. Department of War, DOW）于 2026 年 7 月 10 日（星期五）发布了其最新一批与"不明异常现象"（unidentified anomalous phenomena, UAP）相关的影像与其他资料。这是战争部所谓"总统 UAP 事件解封与报告系统"（Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters，PURSUE）框架下的第四次正式发布，此次公开的档案中包括数段此前从未公开的美国军方视频，画面显示不明空中物体，均由五角大楼下属的"全域异常解决办公室"（All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office，AARO）获得。

"今天，战争部正在发布 PURSUE 框架下第四批已解密与历史性的 UAP 档案，"主管公共事务的战争部部长助理、首席五角大楼发言人肖恩·帕内尔（Sean Parnell）表示。

"该资料集将继续存放于 WAR.GOV/UFO 网站，战争部将以滚动方式陆续发布更多文件，"帕内尔补充说。

### 本次发布包含哪些内容

新视频画面显示的是小而模糊的物体，由战斗机或无人机（unmanned aerial vehicles, UAVs）搭载的多传感器光电瞄准系统拍摄，个别情况下则来自民航飞机所载成像系统。到目前为止，PURSUE 计划公开的所有视频与其他资料都没有提供清晰的、可归因于未知飞行器或表现出不寻常/先进技术的物体样本。

其中真正属于全新公开的内容大多集中于"未解 UAP 案件"——即因数据不足或其他条件限制，政府仍无法判定所观察到的现象为何物的事件。

除视频外，本次发布还附带若干最早可追溯至 1940 年代的历史文件，其中多数曾经由不同美国政府机构在此前批次中公开过。

### 值得关注的案例

在第四批中列出的若干未解报告包括：

- **"DOW-UAP-PR030，2023 年中东地区未解 UAP 报告"**：画面中两处对比明显的目标穿越传感器视场。
- **"DOW-UAP-PR112，2019 年美国东部未解 UAP 报告"**：目击者据报告描述该物体所展示的"飞行特性完全不同于其在美空军服役 28 年间见过的任何东西"。
- **"DOW-UAP-PR116，2020 年大西洋上空未解 UAP 报告"**：描述称目标呈"较深的暗红棕色，高约 12 至 15 英尺（约 3.6–4.6 米）"，外观类似"一只大而有些变形的气球"。

据报道拍摄于 2020 年美国西部上空的画面尤其值得关注，因为它与 2004 年由 F/A-18"超级大黄蜂"（Super Hornet）飞行员查德·安德伍德（Chad Underwood）在下加利福尼亚（Baja California）海岸外拍摄的著名"Tic Tac"影像颇为相似。然而，与近期 PURSUE 其他批次一样，这段影像并未提供更多背景信息，物体究竟为何物仍不清楚。

本次 PURSUE 发布中附带的一组 NASA 历史图像来源于 1996 年的 STS-80 任务，为哥伦比亚号（Columbia）航天飞机机组人员在轨拍摄，画面上呈现出一个小型的三角形或锥形物体。1996 年 11 月 19 日至 12 月 7 日的 STS-80 任务期间，航天员先后拍下了三张涉及一个不明物体的低地球轨道图像，第一帧中该物体位于画面近中央、地球边缘（limb）右侧位置。图中物体到底是什么尚不清楚，尽管 STS-80 任务中据称发生过多起"不寻常现象"的观察。对此类物体最常见的解释是：它们其实是从航天飞机排出的冰晶颗粒，其高反射表面在航天飞机相机所捕捉的光学条件下容易形成上述视觉效果。

### 后续会如何

档案集将继续存放于 WAR.GOV/UFO，战争部将以滚动方式陆续释出更多文件。战争部及其合作机构"正在积极准备下一批 UAP 档案的发布"，预计未来数周内将上线战争部官网。

自 WAR.GOV/UFO 于 2026 年 5 月 8 日上线以来，该网站已在全球累计获得逾 17 亿次访问。
