**Section:** Style

**Byline:** Monica Hesse

**Publication date:** June 18, 2026 (The Washington Post)

**Lede / available excerpts:**

The federal government had spent decades dismissing people who believe in aliens, but the Trump administration has gestured toward taking the issue seriously. Last month the White House launched a website called aliens.gov, which scores of believers immediately clicked on.

Some in the UAP community are gratified by recent pop‑culture attention — but they aren't happy with the government's use of it.

**Available reporting points:**

- The administration has, since spring 2026, branded a wave of activity as a kind of "Disclosure Day" — registering the **aliens.gov** domain, opening the **WAR.GOV/UFO** site, and releasing three batches of declassified UAP records under the "Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters" (PURSUE).
- The June 12 third batch — 53 documents, 10 images, six videos and three NASA audio recordings — contained material that long‑time UFO observers found underwhelming: spinning discs, glowing orbs, a "potato‑shaped" object over the Cheyenne Mountains, and the 2008 Harare International Airport sighting transmitted to the White House Situation Room.
- UFO watchers and disclosure advocates the piece quotes (or paraphrases) say the rollout has produced no "blockbuster" revelation that matches the President's repeated teases, and that aliens.gov in particular — which uses extraterrestrial imagery to spotlight immigration enforcement against undocumented "aliens" — feels to them like a bait‑and‑switch.
- The article contrasts this frustration with the wave of mainstream pop‑culture interest (Steven Spielberg's "Disclosure Day," the Capitol Hill "Disclosure Day" rally on June 9 organized around David Grusch and Reps. Burchett, Burlison, Moskowitz and Luna), arguing that disclosure has become a cultural moment without yet becoming a substantive political deliverable.
- The piece is framed as a Style‑section read of the disclosure community's mood: gratified to be taken seriously after decades on the fringe, but suspicious that the administration is monetising their language for political messaging without producing the documentary "smoking gun" they have been promised.
